## What "best VPS for TikTok" actually means

The ranking pages for this keyword compare the same four things: vCPU count, RAM, disk and monthly traffic. HostAdvice's November 2026 list of eight providers is typical. It defines a TikTok VPS as hosting "optimized for running TikTok-related apps, bots, or websites," then scores uptime, storage, scalability and price. IP type doesn't appear anywhere in the criteria, and the cheapest entries start between $2.00 and $5.52 per month in USD for datacenter capacity with no stated IP provenance.

That definition fits a website or a scraper. It doesn't fit an account you care about. TikTok's risk system scores the address your connection arrives from, and IPv4 blocks registered to hosting companies score badly next to residential broadband and native commercial ranges. That gap is the entire reason a 2-core box can cost AUD $24.90 at one provider and $4 at another.

A 4-core machine on a flagged datacenter subnet will get an account throttled faster than 1 core on a clean residential range. Read it as a vertical supplier with a vertical price, not as a market-wide winner.

## Using the 3-day / 10GB refund window

Ten gigabytes sounds generous until you do the arithmetic. A 1080p TikTok stream at 4–8 Mbps consumes 10GB in roughly 2.8 to 5.6 hours (10GB is 80 gigabits; 80Gb ÷ 8 Mbps ≈ 10,000 seconds). That's the whole allowance, and the 3-day clock usually expires first anyway.

Spend it in this order:

1. Confirm the IP. Check the address's registration and, more importantly, what region TikTok reports when you log in from the server. A "Singapore" plan that resolves to another country is a refund, not a support ticket.
2. Run one short stream at your real bitrate for 20–30 minutes during your peak audience hours, and watch the dropped-frame counter rather than the preview window.
3. Note the upload headroom. A 200 Mbps port is far above the 4–8 Mbps a 1080p stream needs, so instability usually means routing, not capacity.
4. Keep whatever traffic is left for your first real broadcast.

Don't use the allowance for a stress test. It won't survive one.

## FAQ

**Does a residential IP stop TikTok from limiting reach?**
No. It changes how the connection is classified, which reduces one category of risk. No provider can promise an account won't be throttled, and anyone who does is selling you something they don't control.

**Singapore or the US?**
Match the IP to the audience, not to a benchmark. US-facing streams and TikTok Shop accounts need a US IP; Malaysia, Indonesia and Vietnam need Singapore. Streaming to a US audience from a Singapore box adds latency and looks inconsistent with the account's history.

**Is annual billing worth it?**
20% versus 10% is a real difference, roughly AUD $40 a year on the residential plan. Take it after the test stream, not before, since a region that fails your test is a region you don't want for twelve months.

**How much bandwidth does a TikTok live stream need?**
Around 4–8 Mbps of sustained upload at 1080p with OBS. A few hundred Mbps of port speed is plenty; the traffic cap is what runs out. 1TB is the floor, 2–4TB is comfortable for long or frequent broadcasts.

**Can I stack two promo codes?**
No. One code per order, and the US-only codes don't apply to Singapore.
